01Overview

FourFold is built on one rule: what you write down is yours, and it stays in your own iCloud account. This policy explains exactly what the app touches on your device, what leaves it, and what never does.

Account required

None — no sign-up, no login

Where entries are stored

Your private iCloud database

Analytics or ad tracking

None

Developer access to your entries

Not possible

no analytics SDK no ad network no cross-app tracking no data resale

02Where your entries live

Every To-Do, Decision, Idea, Let Go entry, mood check-in and diary note is stored locally on your device using Apple's SwiftData framework, and synced through your personal iCloud account via CloudKit's private database.

What that means in practice: your data moves between your own devices signed into the same iCloud account, encrypted in transit and at rest by Apple's infrastructure. FourFold has no server of its own, and the developer cannot see, access, or recover your entries — there is no dashboard, database, or backend that any FourFold employee could query.

If you're signed out of iCloud, or have iCloud Drive disabled for the app, your entries stay on-device only and simply don't sync across devices.

03Calendar access

If you turn on "Add to Calendar" for a To-Do or Decision, or "Schedule First Step" for an Idea, FourFold asks for permission to create an event in your device's Calendar app via Apple's EventKit framework. This happens entirely on-device — the event is written straight to your calendar database, and FourFold never transmits it anywhere else.

You can revoke calendar access at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars on your device; existing calendar events aren't deleted automatically if you do.

04Diary photos

Photos you attach to a day's diary entry are stored the same way as your text entries: locally, then synced through your private CloudKit database. FourFold only accesses the specific photo you choose to attach, via the system photo picker — it does not have blanket access to your photo library.

05App lock (Face ID / passcode)

If you enable the passcode lock in Settings, FourFold uses Apple's LocalAuthentication framework to confirm it's you — your device's passcode, Face ID, or Touch ID — before showing your entries. This check happens entirely on-device; no biometric data or passcode is ever seen, stored, or transmitted by FourFold.

06Purchases

FourFold's one-time unlock is processed entirely by Apple through StoreKit. FourFold never sees your payment details, card number, or Apple ID — Apple handles the transaction and simply confirms back to the app that you've purchased. Refunds and billing questions go through Apple, not FourFold directly.

07Sharing & third parties

FourFold does not sell, rent, or share your entries with any third party, for any purpose. There is no advertising SDK, analytics SDK, or crash-reporting service built into the app that would transmit your data off your device.

The only outside party involved at all is Apple, acting as your iCloud and App Store provider — governed by Apple's own privacy policy for those services.

08Keeping or deleting your data

  • Deleting an entry, day, or photo in the app removes it from your device and, once synced, from your iCloud private database.
  • Deleting the FourFold app removes all local data; anything already synced remains in your iCloud account until you clear it via Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage on your device, or it's overwritten by a future sync.
  • The in-app Backup & Restore export creates a file that lives wherever you choose to save it (Files, iCloud Drive, etc.) — FourFold has no copy of it once it's saved.

09Children

FourFold is not directed at children and is not intended for use by anyone under 13. The app collects no personal information from anyone, of any age, since nothing is transmitted to the developer in the first place.

10Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date at the top of this page will change with it. Material changes — anything that alters what data the app touches — will also be noted in the App Store release notes for that update.
